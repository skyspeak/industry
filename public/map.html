How to read this, and the honest caveat. VIX and the S&P are the market factor in two costumes — VIX "wins" any naive horse race simply because equities fall when volatility rises, which is why both are excluded from the "Top macro" column and reported separately below. Copper is the strongest genuine macro driver across most themes, consistent with these being industrial-buildout trades. But note the magnitudes: the best true-macro R² across all ten themes is 0.31, against a market-factor R² reaching 0.62. Strip market beta out and the strongest residual macro relationship falls to 0.037. On this year of data these themes are overwhelmingly beta stories, not macro stories.

Method & Limitations

Rates come from the US Treasury daily par yield curve CSV, not from Yahoo's ^TNX, which returned only 16 usable daily closes over a year. Everything else — volatility, credit, sector and thematic ETFs — is CBOE's delayed daily history. The Nasdaq 100, dollar, crude, gold, copper and bitcoin are shown through the ETFs that track them (QQQ, UUP, USO, GLD, CPER, IBIT), so levels are ETF prices and changes track the underlying net of fees. Credit appetite is the HYG/LQD price ratio, a proxy for the high-yield spread rather than the spread itself.

Factor betas are univariate ordinary least squares on daily returns over the trailing year. Univariate rather than multivariate on purpose: with nine correlated factors and roughly 250 observations, multivariate coefficients are unstable and hard to interpret. The trade-off is that univariate betas do not separate overlapping factors — a copper beta partly reflects the market beta riding underneath it, which is exactly why the residual column exists.

Residual exposure regresses theme returns on the S&P first, then regresses the residuals on each macro factor. That isolates macro sensitivity that is not simply high beta.

These are descriptive, not predictive. A beta measured over one year describes what happened; it does not forecast. Correlations over a single year are unstable, which is what the rolling chart is for. R² is shown on every cell so weak relationships read as weak instead of being quietly presented as signal, and cells where |t| < 2 are dimmed.

Regime analysis is a small sample. One year sliced into direction buckets leaves tens of days per bucket. Signs and ordering are informative; magnitudes are not.

GICS classification is Yahoo's, which occasionally differs from S&P's official assignment. Value-chain layers are an editorial taxonomy — a company's centre of gravity is a judgment call, and the spans 3+ layers flag marks vertical integrators whose single-layer placement understates their reach.

Hard macro is absent. CPI, payrolls, and ISM are not reachable from this environment. Everything here is market-priced and daily, which suits correlating against daily equity returns but omits the official economic series entirely.

Nothing here is investment advice. This page states relationships and leaves the conclusions to the reader by design.
