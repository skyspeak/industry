How it works

  1. Pick. On the first trading day of January, rank every stock in the S&P 500 at the previous year-end by its calendar-year price gain. Buy the top one with the whole balance.
  2. Protect. Set a stop 35% below the purchase price (the Dec 31 close). If the stock trades there at any point in the year, sell at the stop and hold cash until next January.
  3. Rotate. Next January, sell and buy the new winner, even if it is the same stock.

Test period: January 1997 to Oct 26, 2026. Benchmark: SPY with dividends reinvested (2026 so far is price only). Returns are price-only through 2011 and total return from 2012, matching the source exhibits. No taxes or trading costs. The stop sold in 2000, 2002, 2008 and 2011, and has not fired since 2011.

Why 35%

Ending value of $100 at each stop level. Tighter stops sell winners on ordinary dips; looser ones ride each crash further down. 35% sits just past the deepest dip any non-collapse year produced (Reebok, −32.3% in 2001) and short of the shallowest collapse (Netflix, −64.5% in 2011). Faded segments: years without intraday data could pull a tight stop down to the low end.

Before trusting it

  • The 35% level was picked with hindsight. It fits the four crashes in this sample almost perfectly.
  • One stock holds everything. Without the stop, 2002 (Nvidia −83%) or 2008 (NOV −67%) would have wiped out most of the balance.
  • The stop stays at the January price. It does not trail, so it protects none of a big gain made during the year.
  • Gaps. A stock can open far below the stop on bad news. The backtest assumes a clean exit at exactly −35%.
  • Taxes. Every sale is a short-term gain in a taxable account.
  • Data. The race uses today's index members. Pre-2000 years and delisted names come from the source exhibits. The chance of finishing first is a rough estimate, not a forecast.
